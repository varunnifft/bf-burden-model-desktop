5Furnace operation

gas flow above the burden
The gas slows the falling stream and pushes small particles back toward the centre. Extended model only. Defaults are estimates: enter your current operating values.
Which of these change the trajectory?
  • Mean particle size (MPS) of each material: take. It sets the drag on each particle in the gas. Small sizes (nut coke, small pellets, fines) are slowed most and land nearer the centre; 40–60 mm coke hardly moves.
  • Blast volume: take. With oxygen it sets the gas volume: bosh gas ≈ 1.21 × blast + 2 × oxygen. More gas means a faster upward flow above the burden, which pushes against the falling stream.
  • Oxygen flow: take. Each Nm³ of oxygen makes about 2 Nm³ of CO, so enrichment raises the gas volume at the same blast.
  • Top pressure: take. It sets the gas density and the actual gas velocity. Higher top pressure makes the gas denser (more drag on the moving stream) but slower (less upward push); the model weighs both.
  • Top gas temperature: take (added). Needed to turn Nm³ into the actual gas volume at the top.
  • Blast pressure: skip for the trajectory. It acts below the burden; above the stockline only top pressure matters. It is kept to show BP − TP, the pressure drop across the burden, as a permeability check.

How big is it? At usual top-gas velocity (about 1–2 m/s at top pressure) the landing point of 40–60 mm coke and 10–25 mm ore moves by only millimetres to about 2 cm, so the matrix still decides the distribution. The shift grows quickly for fines and sizes under about 10 mm, and at low top pressure. The ring table shows the shift for every ring.

Drag is applied to the free fall from the chute tip to the burden, using a sphere of the material's MPS with particle density = bulk density ÷ 0.55. The ore side uses its mass-weighted size and density. Not included: gas from coal injection or steam (add its volume to blast volume if large), gas channelling, and any effect of MPS on the angle of repose (enter repose in the material table).
